The Wind Route

Cumbuco to Atins. Steady wind from August to January, flat water lagoons, and hundreds of kilometres you can ride in one direction.

Brazil's northeast coast has what kitesurfers travel for: wind that fills in almost every afternoon for months, flat water in the lagoons, and enough coastline to ride in a straight line for days without repeating a beach.

What it does not have is anyone organising the other half in your language. Where the gear comes from. Who drives your bag to the next town. What happens when the wind drops with fifteen kilometres still to go. Which school teaches and which one only sells lessons.

That half is what we do.

the stretch

Six stops, one direction, always downwind

You can ride the whole thing or any piece of it. Most people pick three or four stops and take their time.

Ceará · startCumbuco

Forty minutes from Fortaleza airport. A big lagoon, easy logistics, and where most people land and get their first days in.

CearáPreá

Long flat water and consistent wind, quieter than its famous neighbour and a better place to actually ride all day.

CearáJericoacoara

The lagoon, the dune, the nightlife. Busy, and worth two or three days precisely because it is busy.

CearáCamocim

Where the river meets the sea and the crowd thins out. The stop that turns a trip into an expedition.

Piauí · MaranhãoDelta and Tutóia

The Parnaíba delta, the only open sea delta in the Americas. Mangrove, sandbanks and almost nobody.

Maranhão · endAtins

Wind on one side, the Lençóis dunes on the other. The best possible place to finish a week on the water.

The last stop is why this route works as one trip. You end a week of riding by walking into a desert full of lagoons, with everything already arranged.

two ways in

Whether you are learning or already riding

You want to learn

We place you with a school that teaches properly, in your language where possible, and not the one that pays the largest commission for sending people. Gear included, lessons spread across days so your body keeps up, and a base with flat water and steady wind so you progress instead of fighting conditions.

You already ride

Downwind runs from one town to the next, with a support boat or a vehicle following the coast, a crew that knows where the sandbanks have moved, and your bags waiting at the other end. You ride, we handle the rest.

the downwind

How a day actually goes

01Breakfast, then the forecast together. If the wind is not there, the day moves. Nobody rides a bad day because it was on a schedule.
02Your bag goes by road to that night's town while you are still eating.
03You launch in the early afternoon, when the wind fills in. Support follows along the coast the whole way.
04Stops on empty beaches, because the point is not to finish quickly.
05If something breaks or the wind drops, you get picked up. That is what the support is for.
06You land, shower, and the room is already booked. Dinner somewhere worth eating.

Distances run from short hops to long full days, matched to how you ride rather than to a brochure. Tell us honestly what you can do and nobody gets hurt or humiliated.

the wind window

Roughly August to January

The trade winds build through the second half of the year and hold for months. The edges of the window shift every season, so treat this as the shape of it rather than a promise.

Worth knowing: the strongest wind months and the fullest Lençóis lagoons do not fully overlap. If you want both at their best, tell us and we will show you where the compromise sits. It is the sort of thing nobody mentions until the flight is booked.

gear

Bring it or rent it

Renting

Available at every stop on the route. We arrange it in advance, so the kit waiting for you is current and in good condition and you are not negotiating in Portuguese on your first morning.

Bringing your own

Fine, and often cheaper on a longer trip. We will tell you what the domestic airlines actually charge for a board bag and arrange for it to be moved between towns so you never carry it.
